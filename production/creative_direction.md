## Narration direction

The narrator should be a mature, low-to-mid register masculine English voice: warm rather than theatrical, patient and compassionate in human loss, precise in the archaeological section, and quietly firmer for the fall of Troy and the final evidence reveal.

- Dialogue will remain distinct and brief; the narrator is never rushed.
- The delivery should use natural pauses and native prosody rather than artificial pitch shifting, which can sound synthetic and weaken credibility.
- The final act gains force through a slightly more deliberate cadence, reduced music under emotional lines, and a controlled musical lift after the last line—not through a sudden voice-character change.
- The selected narrator will remain consistent across all four parts. Character and mascot voice auditions occur later, after narrator approval and the applicable production gates.

## Voice approval gate

A two-option narrator audition will use this representative text:

> Three thousand years ago, Troy burned. The poets blamed gods, desire, and one impossible choice. But beneath the legend lies a real hill, a ruined city, and a question that has survived every fire: what truly happened there?

No VO clips or media assets will be generated before the user chooses an audition voice.
